Building a sample e-learning course

Move safely is a 10-minute course on safe moving and handling for new support workers in New Zealand. I made it to show how I plan, script, design, build and test e-learning.

Who it's for

New support workers in aged care and disability support. They move objects and people every shift, often in a hurry, and usually learn on a phone between tasks. So the course is short, works on a phone first, and puts one idea on each screen.

The process

  1. PlanI chose the topic and an eight-level outline: why it matters, spotting risks, removing the lift, moving objects, moving people, a scenario and a quiz.
  2. ScriptEvery screen was written before any design. Each fact is tagged to its source, WorkSafe's manual handling pages or a page of ACC's Moving and Handling People guidelines, and checked against it.
  3. DesignI compared seven looks on the same lesson screen and picked a "quest" style, with levels, points and chunky buttons, to keep a dry safety topic moving.
  4. BuildOne screen template reused for every level, with reusable parts: progress bar, back and next buttons, flip cards, a branching scenario and a quiz. All the words live in one data file, so the same template could run a different course.
  5. TestThe whole course was run through with only the keyboard, at phone size, with wrong answers picked on purpose, and checked with Lighthouse.

Decisions I made

  • No TILE. Many manual handling courses teach the TILE checklist, but it comes from UK guidance. WorkSafe New Zealand groups risks into five kinds instead, so the course teaches those.
  • Checked sources, not memory. Research turned up that one WorkSafe guide on moving people in healthcare has been withdrawn, so the course doesn't rely on it.
  • Gates, not walls. Learners can't skip ahead, but each gate says exactly what unlocks it ("Flip all five cards").
  • Points for getting it right first time. Quiz answers lock after one try, and the scenario bonus only counts on the first pick, so the score means something.
  • Accessible by default. Real buttons for every interaction, a strong focus outline, spoken feedback for screen readers, and motion turned off for people who ask their device for less.

Tools, and how I used AI

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, hosted on Netlify. I worked with an AI assistant (Claude). I made the calls on topic, outline, look and every change, and the AI drafted the script and code from my direction. Every fact was checked against the WorkSafe and ACC sources listed on each screen, and I reviewed the result before it went live.

What I'd improve next

  • Package it as SCORM so it can run inside an LMS like Moodle and report scores back.
  • Add audio narration and real photos of equipment such as hoists and slide sheets.
  • Test it with a few real support workers and rewrite anything they stumble on.

Sample only
